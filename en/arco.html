Legal Operations

caseload ≠ procedure

At the appellate level there was a caseload, deadlines and a panel, but no established procedure and no tradition to fall back on.

The system

ECHO, designed in 30 days: intake, classification, distribution, verification, production and chambers memory, in reusable materials.

What remained

The model passed to the chambers that took over the operation. Every number on the sheet has a source or calculation declared in the trace.

Open ECHO

Read the origin

The first movement took place in first-instance chambers, when I began coordinating a legal operation whose high volume, combined with an intake of 36,000 cases, required making the caseload legible without letting the portfolio pile up. The procedure was replicated in another unit, with the training of a new chambers team.

In the move to the appellate level, the first diagnosis was silence. There was a caseload, there were deadlines, there was a panel. There was no established procedure. The only standard available was the first-instance one, and it did not come up along with us.

That absence made the problem clear: there was no common practice yet. It remained to define how a case came in, how it was classified, how it was assigned and how it was written, with no tradition to draw on and no one inside who could answer.

In 30 days, I developed ECHO, a legal methodology for high-volume operations in appellate chambers, with reusable materials that structure intake, classification, assignment, verification, production and memory.

Formed by echoes, it echoed too: a model handed on to the chambers that took over the operation, about to deal with what had once been taken for noise.

Legal Engineering

draft ≠ formulate

A sudden shift in the indicators showed output accelerating out of step with the method, as the corrections queue indicated.

The system

ATRIO governs AI-mediated legal reasoning across memory, formulation, confrontation and data protection. The decision remains human.

What it measured

A corpus of 115,114 TJPR case records and an API with 31 routes and 188 automated tests.

Open ATRIO

Read the origin

While a new working methodology was being rolled out, a sudden shift in the indicators revealed that output was accelerating out of step with method, as the stream of corrections showed.

That asymmetry made it clear that artificial intelligence could not be treated merely as a drafting tool. Once inside the legal workflow, it changed how documents were read, how hypotheses were formulated, how legal grounds were organized, and how the text was finished.

In response, I designed ATRIO: an architecture for governing AI-mediated legal reasoning, spanning memory, formulation, confrontation and data protection.

AI does not replace the decision: it operates along a governed path.

    produce ≠ verify

    Output accelerated, and the corrections queue came to concentrate the cost of reviewing AI-mediated reasoning.

    The system

    Independent lenses examine the ruling’s premises, inferences and grounds before the fluency of the text hides the flaw.

    What it measured

    15 of 17 rulings completed on the first attempt. In 9 of 12 rulings, at least one question on a point open to a motion for clarification; in 7, on the point later corrected.

    Open CERNE

    Read the origin

    Rolling out a new working methodology brought productivity gains, but it exposed a new bottleneck: while the clerks’ output sped up, the corrections queue concentrated the cost of reviewing AI-mediated legal reasoning.

    The problem no longer lay in the capacity to produce, but in the capacity to verify. Individual review, carried out at the end of the workflow, had to identify weaknesses that the fluency of the text could conceal.

    In response, I conceived CERNE: a system for the critical audit of legal reasoning, structured in independent philosophical lenses that examine premises, inferences, grounds and argumentative vulnerabilities.

    Managing productivity also required governing the quality of what was being produced.

    Foundation

    Legal Reasoning Governance is a conceptual delimitation of my own authorship, formulated and developed in a series of five texts.

    AI, data, and organizational governance frameworks adopt distinct units of control. Here, the unit is the conclusion with legal consequences and the path that made it possible.

    Canonical formulation

    A discipline that establishes and controls the conditions under which technologically mediated reasoning is formed when it supports conclusions with legal consequences.

    Discipline names both the field that examines these conditions and the regime that institutes them.

    In operational terms, trace and grounding are necessary but not sufficient conditions for governance.

    Trace
    The record the system leaves, from which the path can be reconstructed.
    Grounding
    The verifiable basis for what was stated along the way.

    The mean across two flows conceals the difference between them.
