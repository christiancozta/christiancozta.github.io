01

Foundation

The first movement took place in a first-instance tax enforcement court, facing a high-volume caseload built around highly repetitive case patterns. The work involved triage, workload management, backlog remediation, and the digital integration of 36,000 tax enforcement cases. That is where the initial insight took shape: before accelerating legal output, the caseload had to be made legible and the work measurable. DATA would emerge from there.

02

Direction

The second movement matured at the appellate level, with a shift from managing volume to directing the decision workflow. With no established procedure and no one inside to ask, emerged as a method for structuring the operation: caseload organization, thematic segmentation, routine standardization, priority control, team management, and quality control for draft decisions. This is where judicial experience becomes a legal operations method.

03

Architecture

The third movement begins when method meets artificial intelligence and reasoning starts to accelerate before the method does. turns the logic already built through ECHO into a modular architecture, with a document corpus, assisted reasoning, human validation, traceability, and quality control. AI does not replace judgment: it operates within a governed path.

Data Engineering & Corpus Architecture

4
Data Ingestion & API Integration Acquire and persist documents through a reproducible, resilient pipeline in formats suitable for processing. ATRIO CORPUS

Problem addressed

The documents exist, but cannot be acquired in a stable, reproducible manner suitable for processing at scale.

Implemented mechanism

Document acquisition pipeline with querying, ingestion, parsing, and incremental persistence. The implementation evolved from web-interface automation to use of an official structured data source, with resumable execution and storage in a processable format.

Evidence

  • reverse engineering of the case-law portal;
  • initial automation in Selenium;
  • migration to the public DataJud/CNJ API;
  • ingestion from a CSV dataset;
  • structured query filters;
  • incremental persistence in JSONL;
  • resumption of interrupted execution;
  • corpus of more than 115,000 decisions.

Data Quality & Normalization Prepare the corpus through deduplication, cleaning, standardization, and structural validation. ATRIO CORPUS

Problem addressed

Document repositories accumulate duplicates, inconsistent formats, incomplete structures, and noise. Failure at intake contaminates retrieval, analysis, and generation.

Implemented mechanism

Upstream preparation of the corpus before use by downstream components. Records are deduplicated, structures are corrected, and documents are normalized according to the intended processing purpose.

Evidence

  • deduplication of documents from multiple sources;
  • textual and structural normalization;
  • decision parsing;
  • integrity validation;
  • selection and documentation of the dataset used.

Decision Systems & Automation

4
Adversarial Evaluation Subject formulations to critical challenge using distinct reasoning logics before delivery. ATRIO CERNE

Problem addressed

A formulation may contain an error, omission, or weak grounds that a confirmation-oriented review fails to detect.

Implemented mechanism

Critical review using reasoning distinct from the reasoning that produced the initial formulation. The output is deliberately stress-tested, compared, and evaluated against defined legal and informational criteria.

Evidence

  • comparative rounds A, B, and C;
  • comparison between models and reasoning strategies;
  • criteria of coherence, support, and relevance;
  • hallucination control;
  • logging of results;
  • legal evaluation of the outputs produced.

Associated correspondences

Controlled Generation Refine language and structure without reopening validated legal content. ATRIO LUX

Problem addressed

The legal content has already been validated, but the output may still vary in language, structure, or length and, during refinement, improperly reopen the merits.

Implemented mechanism

Textual refinement after validation, subject to explicit constraints on language, structure, and scope of intervention. The module may improve the wording, but cannot silently alter approved legal content.

Evidence

  • executable application of the Style Guide;
  • output constraints;
  • controlled vocabulary;
  • preservation of validated content;
  • separation between textual refinement and legal decision-making;
  • a second layer of protection over the output.

Implemented mechanism

Decomposition of legal formulation into verifiable stages, with bounded context, modular instructions, critical lenses, and formal progression criteria. The system assists the construction of the decision without being given authority to decide.

3
Human-in-the-Loop & Quality Gates Prevent automatic progression through sensitive stages without human validation and accountability. ATRIO RATIOCERNE

Problem addressed

Automation may advance through a sensitive stage without the requirements having been met or without the operator exercising independent judgment.

Implemented mechanism

Mandatory human interventions and transition controls distributed throughout the path. Progression depends on compliance with legal and operational criteria, and the final decision remains reserved to the operator.

Evidence

  • human gates;
  • formal progression criteria;
  • interruption of progression in the event of failure;
  • intermediate validations;
  • assignment of roles by stage;
  • reservation of the final decision;
  • integration of adversarial evaluation as a critical control.

Privacy by Design & Pseudonymization Protect personal data before input and control its exposure in generated output. ATRIO CORPUSLUX

Problem addressed

Real legal documents contain personal data and sensitive information that cannot be indiscriminately exposed to models or work repositories.

Implemented mechanism

Two-layer pseudonymization, with consistent replacement of entities by stable codes, a controlled external mapping, and a dedicated inventory of the transformations performed.

Evidence

  • protection at intake, during corpus acquisition;
  • a second layer of protection on output, during textual refinement;
  • stable codes for each entity;
  • logically separated mapping repository;
  • reversibility only under authorization;
  • records of the categories handled, codes, and human reviews.

When There Was No One to Ask

When I moved to the appellate level, the first diagnosis was silence.

There was a caseload. There were deadlines. There was an appellate panel. But there was no established procedure. The available standard came from the first instance, and it did not carry over.

That absence made the problem clear: the missing layer came before documentation. There was no common practice yet to document. We had to define how cases entered the workflow, how they were classified, how they were assigned, and how drafts were produced, with no established practice to fall back on and no one inside to ask.

Without a standard, the chambers could produce volume without traceability, classification without criteria, and coherence by coincidence.

The two possible sources were external: interviews with other judges' chambers and analysis of the decision corpus. One oral route, one documentary route.

ECHO emerged from what came back from those two inquiries and exists to turn external answers into documented processes, defined roles, control criteria, and reusable knowledge, so that the panel's consistency does not depend on who happens to be in the room.

It was not echo as automatic repetition. It was ECHO as inscription. The extra letter works as a deliberate scar, a deviation small enough to preserve the origin and deep enough to show that something had been traversed.

When Reasoning Tried to Accelerate Before Method

The first sign of AI did not come as a project, but as a curve.

During the rollout of a new work methodology, a sudden change in the indicators revealed that a new force had entered the operation. Output was accelerating. The method was not. The metric suggested improvement; review revealed asymmetry.

It was in that mismatch that the problem became clear: artificial intelligence could not be treated merely as a drafting tool. Once inside the legal workflow, it changed how documents were read, hypotheses were formulated, legal grounds were organized, and texts were finalized.

Without architecture, acceleration could produce fluency without grounding, volume without traceability, and human review reduced to final sign-off.

ATRIO emerged from that tension: governing the path between memory, reasoning, critique, and form, so that productivity meant more than speed: decision support with method, control, and accountability.

Architecture does not replace judgment. It gives form to the deliverable.
